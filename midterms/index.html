A NOTE ON THE NUMBERS

Good maps start
with honest numbers.

This atlas connects elections with political advertising. The overall view combines covered USD spending across Meta, Google and Snapchat. Weekly and campaign totals are prorated to a shared rolling window. Geographic and per-resident amounts are estimates; sources and assumptions are shown below.

What the Meta source tells us

Meta reports targeting criteria and spending at advertiser level. A place named in targeting is an intended audience, not proof that an ad was delivered there. The source does not connect each individual ad to each ZIP area.

How we allocate a budget

Repeated instances of the same positive geographic criterion are collapsed using their maximum reported weight. For each advertiser, we divide each criterion’s weight by the greater of one or the sum of all positive geographic weights. We multiply that share by the advertiser’s reported budget. This produces a bounded allocation scenario and prevents overlapping criteria from duplicating the budget.

criterion weight ÷ max(1, total weights)
× advertiser budget = criterion allocation

ZIP criteria go directly to the corresponding Census ZIP area. Missing spending weights receive no allocation. Only accounts reporting in USD enter the dollar model. Broader local targets use 2020 ACS population weights across ZIP areas in a geographic crosswalk. Unmapped and foreign targets keep their allocation; we never redistribute it to the places we can map.

Two views of geography

All local targets includes mapped ZIP, city, county, state and congressional district criteria. The city and county crosswalk uses 2020 Census areas and representative ZIP centroids; it is approximate. The supplied targeting source does not expose radius geometry. Reported ZIP targets only retains only explicitly named, positive ZIP criteria.

The overall view includes the nationwide population model by default; the Meta source view starts with local criteria. Including nationwide targets distributes their modelled allocation by ZIP-area population. This is a comparison scenario, not measured nationwide delivery.

Boundaries have a date

ZIP areas use Census 2020 ZCTAs, approximations of postal ZIP codes. Some postal codes have no ZCTA. House districts use the Census 120th Congress, January 1, 2026 layer. Changes after that vintage may not be represented. A ZIP can overlap several districts: its allocation is divided by polygon intersection area, which assumes uniform allocation within the ZIP. State amounts use those same area shares.

State legislatures

State Senate and State House layers use 2026 Census legislative boundaries. Their advertising estimates allocate ZIP budgets by the ZIP representative point. A ZIP can span several legislative districts; this view is approximate and uses a different crosswalk from the House area-overlap model.

Candidate records & source coverage

The directory combines official November candidate lists, refreshed federal and statewide rosters from The Green Papers, additional local records from The Ballot Brief, We Vote profiles and portraits, FEC filings and finance summaries, and NGA governor records. Official-list badges apply only to names read from an election office’s general list. Other November records retain their roster or provider association. Earlier primary records and federal filings have separate labels. Local coverage varies, and a ZIP search does not establish a complete personal ballot.

Connecting candidates and accounts

Account links use a reported Facebook page ID, an exact unique name with matching affiliation, or an exact reported ad disclaimer matched to an FEC principal or authorised committee linked to one 2026 candidate ID. The match and evidence appear on each candidate profile. Coverage is partial: no matched account does not mean no advertising.

A reported funder can also match a unique name in the FEC committee registry. PACs and party committees keep their own identities. This funder lookup does not assign their ads to a candidate.

Three different spending figures

Race profiles combine reported Meta account budgets with Google and Snapchat period estimates across all audiences, then show the geographic model’s allocation to the district or state. Those figures use the selected seven- or 30-day historical snapshot. FEC campaign finances are cumulative cycle totals with their own reporting dates and may include committee transfers. They are not added to advertising totals.

Political scope and researched advertisers

Research of the remaining accounts over $10,000 distinguishes election campaigns, PACs, ballot campaigns and issue advocacy from clearly non-political activity. Identified commercial or charitable activity is tagged and excluded by default. Mixed corporate or charity advocacy stays included or unresolved. The exclusion applies to the account-level model; it is not a claim that every historical creative has been individually classified. Use the explicit non-political option for an audit view, and inspect sources in Identity review.

Inferred identity and affiliation

Unclassified accounts may enter party views through a separately labelled identity overlay at ≥95% evidence strength. Scores are rule-based, not calibrated probabilities or authentication guarantees. Numeric candidate page IDs, authorised committees and distinct name–payer corroboration provide evidence. Name-only matches stay withheld. Declared funders do not establish page ownership. Historical views apply current identity evidence only where the historical name and payer still agree. Original tags and budgets are preserved.

Affiliation is a source tag

The uploaded WhoTargets.Me export’s “All US” taxonomy supplies the primary classification. Democrats, Democratic PACs and progressive organisations are grouped as Democratic aligned; Republicans, Republican PACs and conservative organisations as Republican aligned. Other tagged groups stay Other. The overall view includes unclassified accounts by default. The Meta source view starts with tagged accounts; the filter can include unclassified accounts in either view. Additional annotations are preserved separately.

Spending associated with audience criteria

The Targeting view multiplies each reported Meta criterion spending share by the account’s reported USD budget. It retains the largest repeated share for the same criterion and account. Interests, ages and audiences overlap, so their dollar amounts must not be summed. Party budget shares compare that associated amount with each party’s covered Meta budget. For places, account-wide criterion shares are applied to modelled local account allocations; this does not establish which criteria were used by ads delivered there. Exclusions display account counts only when a positive spending share or ad count establishes use. Zero-use source placeholders are omitted, and exclusions receive no positive spending attribution. Detailed criteria coverage is Meta only.

Exclusions and unknowns

Excluded criteria are never counted as positive targets. An advertiser may include and exclude the same place in different ads. Without ad-level links, we cannot subtract an exclusion from all of that advertiser’s campaigns. Advertiser details retain those exclusions. Broad targeting does not prove that there were no other audience settings, or that the algorithm alone determined delivery.
